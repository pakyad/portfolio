# Failures & Lessons

*Honest reflections on things that went wrong. Used to show growth and authenticity.*

## Philosophy
Iyad doesn't treat failures as trophies to display or things to hide. They're evidence of what didn't work and why — the most useful information when you're building something new.

The pattern isn't "I failed and learned." It's: **I tried something, it didn't work, I understood why, and that understanding changed how I approach similar problems.**

## Recurring Themes
* **Over-engineering before understanding the problem** — Building architecture for features that might get deleted
* **Persisting on the wrong approach** — Confusing stubbornness with persistence
* **Under-communicating** — Assuming context is shared when it isn't
* **Ignoring the "why" behind a pattern** — Following best practices without understanding when they stop applying

## How I Fail Now vs Before
Earlier: Failed quietly, didn't document, repeated similar mistakes.
Now: Fail faster, fail smaller, document the lesson, share it so others don't hit the same wall.

## Failure as Portfolio Signal
Including failures in the portfolio isn't about vulnerability for its own sake. It signals: honesty, self-awareness, growth mindset. Real engineering involves things breaking. The interesting part is what happened next.

## Specific Lessons (Kept Private)
Detailed failures and their specific lessons are kept as personal reference rather than public content. The portfolio demonstrates the *results* of those lessons — the architecture choices, the design decisions, the technical debt approach — without needing to catalogue every misstep.
