## Preferred Work
Iyad is particularly interested in frontend development because it sits at a nice intersection between his Software Engineering background and his interest in visual design, interaction and user experience.

However, he doesn't want to be boxed into frontend only. He wants exposure to:
* Backend development
* APIs
* Databases
* Software architecture
* Testing
* Deployment
* Code reviews
* Real development workflows
* Working within an engineering team

The ideal role isn't necessarily one where he gets to do everything from day one. He mainly wants to be somewhere he can gradually understand how real software is designed, built, reviewed, shipped and maintained.

## Preferred Company / Environment
Iyad is particularly interested in established companies where he can learn from experienced engineers and work on real systems rather than being given purely administrative or repetitive internship tasks.

He values:
* Good mentorship
* Room to ask questions
* Some autonomy
* Real engineering work
* People who care about doing things properly
* An environment where disagreement or questioning isn't automatically seen as a problem

He doesn't expect an internship to be completely free-form. He wants guidance, but also wants enough responsibility to actually learn through doing.

## Longer-Term Direction
Iyad's career direction sits somewhere around the intersection of **software engineering, frontend development, product thinking and digital experience design**.

He wants to become capable of taking a problem from:
**idea → UX → interface → engineering → deployment**

rather than being limited to one narrow part of the development process.

He doesn't necessarily know exactly what job title that eventually turns into, and that's fine. The important direction is becoming someone who can understand both **what should be built and how to build it well.**

## What He Wants to Learn
Backend depth, systems design, performance engineering, working within a real engineering team, code review culture, deployment pipelines, and how products evolve after launch.

## 5-Year Vision
Becoming someone who can take a problem from idea through to deployed product — understanding both what should be built and how to build it well. Not necessarily a specific title, but capability across the full spectrum.

## Non-Negotiables
Good mentorship, room to ask questions, real engineering work, people who care about doing things properly, environment where disagreement isn't a problem.

## Red Flags
Purely administrative tasks, no code review, legacy stack only, no learning culture, micromanagement.
